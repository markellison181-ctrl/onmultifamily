**CMHC's Big News**

CMHC - Canada's largest mortgage insurer - doesn't broadcast its policy changes. 

Instead, they update their "approved lenders" list. So if you haven't heard this news yet, you're not alone.

But here's what we know so far:

1. CMHC will *only* offer takeout financing for bridge loans *if* the bridge lender is CMHC-approved.

2. All bridge loans now have to be in place for a minimum of 24 months (unless the submission to CMHC was made prior to the closing date of the acquisition).

To be more clear - I'll use an example. 

In the past - you buy an old apartment building with short-term financing. You might use a private lender in the short term to fix up the building and raise the rents. In 18 months, you plan to receive long-term takeout financing from CMHC so you can take some of your equity out and move on.

Now you can't do that - unless your original bridge lender was CMHC-approved. You also can't refinance to CMHC until after year 2.

Essentially, you'll be fine if your original loan was through a major bank, or the big boys like First National, Kingsett, Peakhill etc.

However, if your bridge loan is through a mortgage investment corp (MIC), private lender, or alternative institution - it may be time to call them.

**Path Forward & Outstanding Questions**

To chart the path forward, it's important to understand CMHC's intent.

They're invoking this rule change to stifle renovictions and apartment flippers. Fair enough.

And - generally - if you're investing in the spirit that CMHC promotes, there's wiggle room in their policies. So don't panic.

But here's where we're still lacking clarity.

Do these decisions take effect immediately across the board? 

Most importantly - are those with VTBs (vendor takeback mortgages) affected? Because *technically* your VTB is just a private mortgage from an unapproved lender.
