# Rental Protection Fund (Taking Applications)

On March 7th, Ottawa announced it will be taking applications for its Rental Protection Fund (from now until May 21, 2025). This is a $1.47 billion initiative (with $470 million in non-repayable contributions / free equity) to support community housing providers in acquiring existing, privately-owned, multifamily.

Nathaniel Erskine-Smith, Canada's Minister of Housing, Infrastructure and Communities.

Most people aren't aware of this, but non-profits have become a significant percentage of the buyer pool. In Toronto Proper, for instance, non-profits have represented 11.23% of multifamily acquisitions since January 1, 2024 ($140M). Their money is real, and they *actually* do deals (albeit on a longer timeline than private investors). 

The fund is to be dispersed over 5 years, so here's how I see it impacting our Ontario market:

Toronto and Vancouver are the least affordable cities in Canada, and the most populous. I would estimate the GTA collects 25% of this grant money. 

- 25% x $470M / 5 years = $23,500,000 in multifamily grants/year

A typical loan-to-value hovers around 70%, so they should have capacity to buy an additional **$78.3M across the GTA every year, representing an extra 3.6%** of the total deal volume for the GTA last year. 

This is a long winded way of saying non-profit share of acquisition **could grow to roughly 14%** of the market (all things being equal). Sellers would be wise to take these organizations seriously.

# Multifamily Market Sentiment

The environment for investors is shifting every day.

Mark Carney is calling an election this Sunday (insiders tell me), we're in the middle of a tariff war, and rents fell nationally for the fifth straight month. It's an environment with a lot of uncertainty.

On the flip side, bond yields are down significantly, meaning a buyer's equity can still stretch enough to meet an owner's expectations.

Deals *are *happening. To get deals done, your buyer needs to have healthy access to equity and strong conviction. There are still TONS of these buyers out there (we speak with them every day).

Who fits this description today? Private buyers - and they're hungry.

I give this market a "B". Liquidity exists for realistic owners, but not sellers seeking that diamond in the rough buyer from 2021. This is my near and medium term outlook, and it's healthy for the market.
