# Capital Gains Tax Deferral Announcement

Last Friday, the Federal Government announced the **deferral** of Canada’s capital gains tax increase from **June 25, 2024** to **January 1, 2026**. This is welcome news for multifamily vendors. Reprieve from a policy that - on it's face was ridiculous.

**Simple Example of Impact**

To illustrate how the original proposal affected owners, let's imagine one of my typical clients. We'll call him Tony.

Tony has owned a 66 unit apartment building since 1995. He's depreciated the asset to (basically) zero over the years. He originally bought it for $1,000,000, but he's considering an offer Colliers brought him for $20,000,000. 

**Implications for a Top-Bracket Taxpayer with a $20M Fully Depreciated Asset:**

Under the Proposed Hike (If It Had Gone Into Effect):

**Capital Gain:** $20M

**Taxable Portion:** 66.7% of $20M ≈ $13.33M

**Tax Liability:** At a top marginal rate (assumed ~50%), you’d pay about $6.67M in taxes.

Under the Current Law (With the Delay):

**Capital Gain:** $20M

**Taxable Portion: **50% of $20M = $10M

**Tax Liability: **At the same top rate, you’d owe about $5M in taxes.

So - with the stroke of a pen, Tony now walks away with an extra **$1,670,000**. This delta is material, and often is the difference between doing a deal, or waiting for an election.

**June Market Reaction**

We know this to be true because in the week leading up to the original proposed date (June 25, 2025), many clients opted to jam deals through to avoid the tax increase. In Q2, 2024 in Toronto for instance - **14 of the 21 transactions** occurred within one week of the June 25th deadline.

**Election Outlook and Future Supply**

Contrary to the polls, I expect a tight election. If the** Liberals** remain in power, there's a chance this policy gets pushed through. In that scenario - there would be a flood of multifamily properties as sellers rush to avoid the higher tax. This supply could hinder pricing in Q4, 2025.

At risk of speaking my book - now would not be a bad time to sell to avoid this.

# Trade War Turbulence: Brace Yourself

As of Monday at 4:36 PM, Trudeau tweeted that the decision on U.S. **tariffs** has been **temporarily delayed until March 1.** It's challenging to fully capture the implications of this policy in a short email, but I'll do my best.

*“If these output losses translate proportionally to employment, Canada could see roughly 600,000 fewer jobs, potentially pushing the unemployment rate up to nearly 10 percent.” — @trevortombe*

Implications of Tariffs If Implemented

**Impact on New Multifamily Developments**

For multifamily builders, **hard costs** could surge by **5–10% (or more)**. Such an increase may render new projects financially infeasible, particularly when economic conditions weaken. In a recession, achieving pro forma rents of—say, **$4.75 per unit**—becomes increasingly challenging.

**Effects on Legacy Multifamily Assets**

While legacy multifamily properties, which constitute the bulk of transactions in Ontario, will be less affected, the market will still face headwinds. Many of my clients have *already* reported a tougher renter market due to **changing immigration policies** and a **struggling economy**. Although the impact won't cause severe pain in most cases, it will likely be an operational challenge. A real thorn in the side for owners.

# Bond Yields: A Silver Lining

The one shining light in the shadow if this trade war: bond yields have plummeted. We haven't seen rates drop this fast since COVID.

In the wake of Trump's tariff announcement, bonds experienced a dramatic drop. Over the past **20 days**, the **CMB-5 Year yield**—a key benchmark for multifamily financing—fell by** 66 basis points**, from **3.50% to 2.94%**. 

As a result, **CMHC financing rates** for a 5-year loan have dropped from roughly **4.00% to about 3.44% **(not exact as spreads have widened). Although I expect continued volatility (i.e. bond yields could shoot up if this trade war is solved), this spread makes the uncertainty palatable.
