### 1. **Interest Rates: Pressure to Cut More Aggressively**

The **Bank of Canada** is under pressure to accelerate rate cuts, possibly by **50 basis points**.

Inflation has been **below target** for most of the year (excluding mortgage costs), raising questions about the central bank’s slow response.

What more evidence do you need? The Bank of Canada needs to get on these rate cuts now," Judge emphasized.

### 2. **Recession or Not? Canada’s Economic Reality**

Canada might already be in a **per capita recession**, with recent growth driven largely by immigration, rather than business investment.

The lack of business investment has been a long-standing issue: "Our entire past economic expansion was driven by debt… We just have not been able to attract business investment."

With unemployment set to rise to **7%**, concerns about the depth of a recession loom.

### 3. **Household Debt: A Ticking Time Bomb**

Many Canadian households are facing significant increases in mortgage payments as loans taken at low rates in **2020-2021** come up for renewal, with monthly payments expected to jump by **15-20%**.

"Excess savings are completely dried up… and that wave of renewals will result in higher payments on the monthly payment of around 15%," which could severely impact consumer spending and the broader economy.

### 4. **Housing Market: Sluggish Response to Rate Cuts**

The Canadian housing market, particularly in **condos and apartments**, is not responding quickly to recent rate cuts.

"You won’t see a drop-off in construction in the multifamily sector yet, but it’s coming… Pre-construction sales are already down heavily," reflecting challenges in attracting new investments.

**Negative cash flow** positions are common for condo investors, further slowing development.

### 5. **Rent Surge Continues: A Long-Term Challenge**

**Rents have risen** 9% year-over-year and are expected to stay high due to limited supply.

Higher interest rates have driven people from the housing market into rentals, but this has not eased affordability pressures.

Despite potential drops in mortgage costs, the rental market will likely continue to see elevated prices well into next year.

### 6. **US Economy: Canada’s Lifeline (and Risk)**

With **75% of Canadian exports** going to the U.S., the health of the American economy is critical for Canada.

The U.S. has shown some economic weakness, but it remains better insulated from global shocks than Canada.

Katherine Judge warned: "If the U.S. goes into a recession, Canada follows." The **differing monetary policies** between the two nations is a risk, though it has not yet hit the Canadian dollar.

### 7. **Population Growth: Fuelling Housing Demand**

Immigration has fuelled demand for housing, but it has also strained supply. Population growth will slow from **3% to 1%** over the next few years, but the **supply shortage** will remain a major issue.

Despite new immigrants creating more consumption, they also add to the labor supply, which can offset inflationary pressures, Judge explained.

### 8. **Wages: No Longer a Threat to Inflation**

Wage growth in key sectors like **construction** has slowed, easing inflation concerns.

"Businesses are reporting that they're not offering big increases. Wage expectations are down, so that threat to inflation has kind of gone away," Judge remarked, suggesting that this trend supports the case for more aggressive rate cuts.

### 9. **Labor Shortages and Productivity in Construction**

Canada’s construction sector is facing productivity challenges due to a shortage of trained workers.

The number of **construction workers per site** has steadily declined, leading to slower project completion times.

Judge highlighted, "You’re not getting good matches for the job, so construction productivity is way down."

### 10. **Bank of Canada’s Next Moves: Faster Rate Cuts Are Likely**

The forecast suggests the **Bank of Canada** could bring rates down to **2.25%** by 2026, though this might happen sooner if economic pressures continue.

"The Bank of Canada needs to accelerate rate cuts… and potentially cut even more," said Judge, reinforcing the urgency to act before the economy worsens.

This trajectory should eventually **ease housing market pressures** and stimulate more activity.
